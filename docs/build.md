# Build guide

Read [`safety.md`](safety.md) first. Build and fit the visor only in a well-lit, uncluttered indoor space with another adult present for the initial wear test.

## Stage 1: establish the baseline

1. Record ten seconds of the wearer facing the camera, then turning about 30 degrees left and right. Use the target camera and normal lighting.
2. Assemble the NEO faceguard exactly as its manufacturer instructs.
3. Inspect it unworn. Reject damaged mesh, cracked carrier parts, burrs, loose hardware, or a headband that does not release normally.
4. Adjust the unmodified guard while seated. Confirm comfortable clearance from nose, cheeks, glasses, and eyelashes.
5. Complete the visibility and removal checks in [`safety.md`](safety.md) before adding anything.

If the unmodified mesh creates unacceptable dimming, distortion, headache, or balance problems, stop. The overlay cannot improve visibility.

## Stage 2: paper mock-up

1. Put the guard on a table, exterior side up. Never cut or mark while it is worn.
2. Trace its usable outer area onto paper. Start with an overlay around `325 x 180 mm`, then trim it clear of the frame controls.
3. Mark a centered eye corridor at least `180 x 60 mm`. Transfer the paper to the unworn guard, then enlarge the opening rather than moving it close to an eye.
4. Print [`panel-template-a4.svg`](../assets/panel-template-a4.svg) at **100% / actual size**. Check that its calibration square is exactly `20 x 20 mm`.
5. Cut the paper panel shapes, clip them to the mock-up, and confirm that they stay above or below the eye corridor and away from the nose/mouth airflow path.
6. Temporarily mount the paper mock-up to the outside perimeter with four loose hook-and-loop loops. With a helper present, repeat the seated visibility check.

Do not continue until the paper version is comfortable and instantly removable.

## Stage 3: textile carrier

1. Transfer the approved paper shape to one layer of stiff black tulle.
2. Cut the outside edge and eye corridor. Overcast or fold-and-stitch every cut edge to prevent fraying.
3. Cut six hook-and-loop straps, initially about `80 mm` each. Round their corners.
4. Sew the midpoint of each strap to the tulle perimeter: two upper, two side, and two lower anchors. The free ends should wrap around only the outer rim or peripheral mesh.
5. Place the carrier on the guard's camera-facing side. Rotate it roughly `5-10 degrees` relative to the steel grid, then fasten the loops without deforming the mesh.
6. Cycle the faceguard angle and head adjustment 20 times. Move or remove any strap that catches a control.

The steel mesh must remain the sole layer across the entire eye corridor. Do not add a second tulle layer there.

## Stage 4: foam facets and reflective accents

1. Trace the approved paper panels onto the black foam and cut them with rounded corners.
2. Punch or pierce sewing holes at least `5 mm` in from each foam edge while the material is lying flat on the table.
3. Sew panels to the exterior tulle carrier. Keep knots and thread ends on the exterior, away from the wearer.
4. Cut reflective tape into small tabs no larger than about `20 x 40 mm`.
5. Stitch the tabs to the outward faces of selected foam panels. Do not place them within the eye corridor or where the wearer can see a direct reflection.
6. Remove the complete carrier, shake it vigorously over a table for 30 seconds, and tug each panel and strap. Repair any loosening before it approaches a face.

## Stage 5: final fit gate

1. Reinstall the carrier on the exterior. Perform a finger sweep over the entire wearer-facing side; there must be no point, wire, stitch tail, or loose part.
2. With the wearer seated and a helper in front, don the visor for one minute and remove it normally.
3. Repeat for ten minutes only if the first minute is symptom-free.
4. Re-run every check in [`safety.md`](safety.md).
5. Weigh the finished overlay if possible and log it. A heavy or front-pulling overlay should be simplified, not counterweighted around the head.

## Stage 6: tune the visual effect

Run the [`test plan`](test-plan.md) before making changes. Change only one variable per round:

1. tulle rotation (`5`, `7`, then `10 degrees`);
2. camera-to-visor distance;
3. small changes to the outer foam silhouette;
4. reflective-tab position for flash tests.

Never tune by shrinking the eye corridor, adding powered lights, adding another sightline layer, or putting reflective material on the wearer-facing side.
